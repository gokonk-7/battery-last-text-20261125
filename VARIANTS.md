# Variants tested

The judge compares entries two at a time and keeps the funnier one, so the
entry had to win on voice, a real punchline in the final words, and an angle
beyond the obvious dying-battery complaint. Six variants were drafted and
scored against the published rubric (on-subject text voice first, then a
turn that pays off at the end, then a human texting voice, then economical
use of the 200 characters, then a fresh angle that survives a second read).

## Chosen

**A (179 chars)** — "i waited all day and you plugged in the laptop first. so i'm spending my last 1% on this: tell the power bank he wins. he charges me in public and never leaves me on 2% overnight."

Why it won: the joke is betrayal, not battery death. The charger is caught
cheating with the laptop, and the battery spends its last 1% delivering a
breakup message through a rival. "He charges me in public" lands the double
meaning and "never leaves me on 2% overnight" is the parting shot. Every
clause earns the punchline; nothing is filler.

## Rejected

**B (158 chars)** — "don't come looking for me. i'm at 1% and spending it on one last thing: telling the group chat you charge his laptop too. i was never your only device, was i?"
Same cheating angle, but the punchline is a question instead of a blow, and
"telling the group chat" is gossip where A has a rival delivering the news.
Weaker final words.

**C (146 chars)** — "last text ever: i held 1% for six hours just to say goodbye properly. you couldn't hold a charge for six minutes. the power bank treats me better."
Funny middle, but "the power bank treats me better" is a shrug, not a
punchline, and "last text ever" announces the joke instead of being the joke.

**D (152 chars)** — "hey. it's 1%. i'm not even mad you left me on the nightstand all day, i'm mad you came back smelling like another phone. tell the outlet i said nothing."
Good voice and a nice closer, but the setup spends half the line on the
nightstand and the payoff ("smelling like another phone") lands mid-line,
so the ending trails off.

**E (166 chars)** — "dear charger, by the time you read this i'll be 0%. please forward my last electron to the flashlight, and tell the user his 47 open tabs did this. love, your battery"
The will/bequest angle is fresh, but "dear charger" reads like a letter, not
a text, and the joke is a cute sentiment with a blame-the-user shrug rather
than a turn that pays off.

**F (182 chars)** — same as A with "never once left me" instead of "never
leaves me". "Once" is filler; A is tighter and the present-tense "leaves"
keeps the sting in the final words.
